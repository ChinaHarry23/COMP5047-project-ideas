# Evidence pack — Catchment bin for plastic-ingesting seabirds

Use this for the Week 4/pivot proposal (short cites) and the final report (full argument). Prefer **Australian necropsy, EPBC, CSIRO, Sydney catchment** over generic “plastic is bad” blogs.

## How to argue (one paragraph you can reuse)

Harmful marine debris is an EPBC Key Threatening Process: vertebrate marine life is injured or killed by ingesting or becoming entangled in it (listed 2003; TAP 2018). Roman et al. (2019) necropsied 1,733 Procellariiform seabirds of 51 species from Australia and New Zealand. 32.1% had ingested debris; 13 deaths were confirmed as debris-caused, mainly gastrointestinal obstruction. Their dose–response model estimates ~20.4% lifetime mortality from one item, 50% at nine, 100% at 93 — a curve fitted to already-dead birds, not a census of the Tasman. Soft items, especially balloons, are disproportionately lethal. Wilcox et al. (2015) place the highest expected risk in Australasia. We do **not** pretend a Camperdown bin feeds shearwaters. We treat silver gulls and ibis as the **vector** that moves high-risk litter toward first-flush drains (Cooks / Harbour), and the shearwater as the **downstream** stakeholder named in the science.

## Core sources (cite these first)

1. **Roman, L., Hardesty, B.D., Hindell, M.A. & Wilcox, C. (2019).** A quantitative analysis linking seabird mortality and marine debris ingestion. *Scientific Reports*, 9, 3202. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-36585-9  
   **1,733** birds, **51** species, Australia + New Zealand (Fraser Island → Macquarie; Ballina NSW → Perth). **557 (32.1%)** ingested debris (1–40 items). **13** known debris deaths (5 fairy prions, 4 short-tailed shearwaters, plus Salvin’s prion, Antarctic prion, blue petrel, light-mantled sooty albatross). Leading mechanism: GI blockage/obstruction. Model: **20.4% / 9 items / 93 items**. Balloons 32× hard plastic. Open access.

2. **Wilcox, C., Van Sebille, E. & Hardesty, B.D. (2015).** Threat of plastic pollution to seabirds is global, pervasive, and increasing. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 112(38), 11899–11904. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1502108112  
   Predicted hotspot: **Australasia**, Tasman Sea / Southern Ocean boundary. 99% of seabird species predicted to ingest debris by 2050 (prediction, not a 2026 measurement).

3. **DCCEEW (2018).** *Threat abatement plan for the impacts of marine debris on the vertebrate wildlife of Australia’s coasts and oceans.*  
   https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/biodiversity/threatened/publications/tap/marine-debris-2018  
   Binds Commonwealth agencies. Actions: reduce input, understand microplastics — **not** “build a bird alarm.”

4. **EPBC Key Threatening Process (2003).** Injury and fatality to vertebrate marine life caused by ingestion of, or entanglement in, harmful marine debris.  
   https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/biodiversity/threatened/key-threatening-processes/harmful-marine-debris

5. **Roman, L., Schuyler, Q.A., Hardesty, B.D. & Townsend, K.A. (2016).** Anthropogenic debris ingestion by avifauna in eastern Australia. *PLoS ONE*, 11. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0158343  
   Eastern Australian birds, including coastal species — supports a **Sydney** sentence.

6. **DCCEEW.** *Impacts of plastic debris on Australian marine wildlife.*  
   https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/marine/marine-pollution/marine-debris/impacts-plastic-debris-australian-marine-wildlife  
   At least **77** marine wildlife species (1974–2008 records), including **≥34 seabirds**. Entanglement often more visible than ingestion.

7. **Carey, M.J. (2011).** Intergenerational transfer of plastic debris by short-tailed shearwaters (*Ardenna tenuirostris*). *Emu*, 111, 229–234. (Phillip Island fledglings — high incidence.)

8. **Cousin, H.R. et al. (2015)** (and later Phillip Island / Tasmania fledgling papers). Use to show the **named species** is local and plastic-loaded; quote the actual year/sample in the report, not from memory.

## What not to over-claim

- Do not say “32% of Australian seabirds are dying of plastic.” 32.1% **had ingested** debris in a **dead-bird** sample; 13/1733 were confirmed debris deaths.
- Do not say “eat 93 pieces and you die on the spot.” 93 is an **extrapolation** (observed loads were 1–40).
- Do not say shearwaters feed at Uni bins. Name gull/ibis as the vector.
- Do not treat Approach 2 as a feral-bird control product. The TAP is **remove high-risk debris**, not broadcast distress.
- Do not make VR the assessed interface.
- Do not claim the prototype reduces Tasman plastic loads in one semester.
